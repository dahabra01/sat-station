The rule

Only as good or better

Every pass gets a rating out of 100. The map keeps the best view of each patch of ground, and stays fresh when a pass is just as good.

Same or higher winsA new pass only replaces part of the map if its rating is the same or higher than the pass already there. Same rating, the newer one goes on top.
Weaker passes waitA pass rated lower never covers up a better one. It only fills ground the map doesn't have yet.
How a pass is ratedSignal 30 how strong it came in · Clean 40 how much of the picture is free of noise and gaps · Length 20 how long it stayed locked · Height 10 how high it climbed.

Behind the map

How it's stitched

1 · Catch the passThe station records a Meteor flyover and turns the radio signal into a picture, a strip of Earth about 2,800 km wide.
2 · Pin it downThe satellite's orbit says where it was every second. Each row of the picture is matched to that moment, then nudged until its coastlines sit exactly on a real coastline map.
3 · Lay it inThe pinned picture goes onto the map following the rule above. Noisy rows and bits that can't be pinned with confidence are left out, never guessed.
