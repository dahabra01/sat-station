Signal is measured as SNR, short for signal-to-noise ratio: how far the satellite stands above the background hiss. Above about 5 dB usually makes a picture.

Reading the pictures

What am I looking at?

One long stripThe satellite photographs a band about 2,800 km wide as it flies, so each capture is a tall strip of Earth, not a square photo.
Different eyesThe camera sees in several kinds of light, some we can't see at all. Each picture type mixes them differently to bring out sea, desert, cloud or heat.
Why the ends fadeThe top and bottom are where the satellite was low on the horizon. The signal is weakest there, so those edges can look noisy or cut off.
